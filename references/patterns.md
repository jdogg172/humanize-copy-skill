# Editing Patterns

Read this for edit or detect-only mode. Patterns are signals to examine, not mechanical bans. Retain a pattern when it is characteristic of the writer, useful in context, and not recurring enough to dominate the piece.

## Sentence-level signals

- Repeated staged antithesis: “not just X, but Y,” “Not because A. Because B.”
- Staccato triples or repeated three-item adjective stacks used for rhythm rather than meaning.
- Decorative em dashes serving the same grammatical function throughout a draft.
- Colon reveals that manufacture significance rather than introduce real information.
- Trailing `-ing` clauses that add vague consequence: “improving outcomes,” “ensuring success.”
- Intensifiers and hedges that avoid a precise claim: `really`, `actually`, `truly`, `simply`, `quite`, `rather`.
- Repeated promotional defaults such as `seamless`, `elevate`, `unlock`, `transform`, `unrivaled`, `meticulous`, `delve`, `leverage`, `harness`, `foster`, `tapestry`, and `testament`.
- Generic attribution: “studies show,” “experts agree,” or “research suggests” without a named source.
- Audience simulation or flattery: “we've all been there,” “you can probably imagine,” “whether you're a startup or a Fortune 500 company.”
- Abstract-noun fog that replaces an observable action, decision, trade-off, or result.
- Synonym cycling that changes the noun while referring to the same thing.
- Symmetric both-sides filler that avoids naming the actual trade-off.

Do not replace these mechanically. Fix the sentence's function: name the fact, subject, action, source, uncertainty, or trade-off. Trade terms and ordinary words such as “leverage” may be correct in context.

## Structural signals

- Universal or cinematic openings unrelated to the actual reason to read.
- Setup followed by three parallel sections and a distilled life lesson.
- Every anecdote followed by an explanation of what it means.
- Tour-guide transitions such as “First,” “Next,” and “This brings us to” where the relationship is not sequential.
- Sections with nearly identical length and internal shape.
- Category coverage included for completeness rather than to test the claim.
- Seamless retrospective certainty that erases what was unknown at the time.
- Repeated recap paragraphs and manufactured profound endings.

Rebuild structure around the real relationship among ideas: chronology, consequence, contradiction, qualification, evidence, decision, or unresolved tension. Unevenness is allowed, but do not force it as a performance of humanity.

## Positive checks

A strong revision normally contains some combination of:

- Concrete facts already present in the source.
- Verbs attached to human or organizational subjects.
- A clear opinion, decision, trade-off, or intended action.
- Sentence and paragraph variation driven by meaning.
- Uncertainty where the writer was genuinely uncertain.
- Channel-appropriate terminology and formatting.

## Ownership check for long-form

Ask what each paragraph contributes that is specific to this writer or this source: evidence, observation, judgment, experience, tension, or necessary context. Revise or remove competent-generic paragraphs, but never invent personal material to make them distinctive.
