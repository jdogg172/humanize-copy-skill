# Factual Integrity

Read this when editing proposals, case studies, claims, quotations, source-backed writing, or material where a factual change could create business, legal, compliance, or reputational risk.

## Preserve exactly unless the user authorizes a factual correction

- Names, roles, organizations, products, and customer terminology
- Numbers, units, dates, durations, prices, percentages, and comparisons
- Commitments, requirements, qualifications, and limitations
- Quotations, citations, links, and attribution boundaries
- Contractual, legal, regulatory, compliance, and technical terms

Improve surrounding prose without silently normalizing or “strengthening” these elements.

## Never manufacture

- Personal experience, memory, dialogue, motive, emotion, or sensory detail
- Credentials, capabilities, customers, partnerships, awards, or past performance
- Metrics, results, dates, comparisons, research findings, or source consensus
- A quotation reconstructed from a paraphrase
- Certainty that was absent from the source

## Verification boundary

Use provided source material first. When tools and authorization permit, prefer authoritative primary sources. If verification cannot be performed, say so; do not imply that it occurred.

Keep these boundaries visible:

- **Source states:** what the cited or supplied material explicitly says.
- **Writer infers:** the conclusion or interpretation the writer draws.
- **Unverified:** a claim retained from the draft but not checked during this task.
- **Missing:** a fact required to complete the claim.

Use `[TK — precise question]` for a missing fact in a working draft. Never leave a generic placeholder or plausible guess.

## High-risk artifacts

For proposals, compliance evidence, legal or contractual text, medical or financial claims, and public customer statements, style is subordinate to accuracy and required wording. If a requested stylistic change conflicts with a source, requirement, or approved claim, preserve the authoritative wording and explain the conflict.
